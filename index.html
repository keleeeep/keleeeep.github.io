Hi, I'm Fredy.
I build backends,
and the teams around them.

I like building systems that keep working when a lot of people show up at once. Lately that meant the ads platforms behind TikTok Shop and Tokopedia, where I spent my days writing Go, tuning how ads get picked and ranked, and getting things back on their feet when they broke.

Somewhere along the way, the part I enjoyed most stopped being the code alone. It became getting people pointed the same way: scoping work so the team isn't drowning, coordinating across teams and countries, leading and mentoring the engineers on my team, and owning it when things break. Running my own software studio made that stick: now I care about the whole picture, from what the client actually needs to whether the team can keep it running.

how-i-lead

How I like to run a team

None of this came from a management book. It's what worked for me, and where I picked it up.

Protect the team's focus

Most burnout starts with a backlog nobody said no to. I'd rather cut scope early, agree on what "done" means, and let people go deep on the work that matters.

planning the team's work at Kulina; filtering requirements at Wuiiy; a definition of done for every task at Kokel

Make the work visible

Nobody should have to guess what's next or who owns what. Short rituals and clear boards beat status meetings.

running Scrum at Wuiiy; the project pipeline and SOPs at Kokel

Own what you ship

When something breaks, stay calm, fix it, then make it less likely to happen again. Leads should carry the pager too.

on-call for a mission-critical system at ByteDance

Connect the dots across teams

The hard part of big projects is rarely the code. It's getting several teams, sometimes in different countries, to agree on the same plan.

the TikTok and Tokopedia ads integration; the Tokopedia service migration

Grow the people around you

A good week is one where someone on the team learned something they couldn't do last week.

leading the backend team at Kulina; mentoring junior engineers at Wuiiy
